BRIEF HISTORY:
The Vision to establish an affordable College in the Municipality of Trinidad was not realized overnight. It took many years of trials, hardships and collective efforts to bring forth what is now popularly known as TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE, one of the fastest growing Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Province of Bohol.
It started in 1980 when Mr. Paciano Petarco initiated the plan for the project of establishing a college institution in the town. He then consulted and sought the help of the Municipal Mayor that time, Atty. Avelino N. Puracan, who consequently assured him to look for competent financier/sponsor to start the project.
Incidentally, the Mayor meet a certain Antero Delos Reyes, a Davao-based educator, a businessman and owner of a private vocational school, in an occasion in Davao where the Mayor was invited as guest speaker by Trinidadnon Association in Davao. He requested Mr. Delos Reyes in putting up a branch of his private vocational school in Trinidad who eventually granted his request. The lone antiquated building in Trinidad, Bohol, used and inaugurated in 1957 as the original Municipal Hall of Trinidad was then converted in the year 1980 into this private school named as Trinidad Institute of Technology (TIT). TIT offered short-term vocational courses, such as Typewriting, Stenography, Tailoring and Bookkeeping.
However, TIT did not last longer than a semester as it “died a natural death”, so to speak. Earnest in their intention to establish a lasting college in the town that would offer affordable yet quality education to students form the lower income families not only from Trinidad but also from the neighboring town did not go in vain. It was in 1985 that Mayor Puracan and Mr. Petarco and the rest of the Municipal Officials at that time spearheaded some affluent Trinidadnons to form themselves as Incorporators, thereby instrumental in the recognition and registration of the TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE with the Securities and Exchange Commission in the same year 1985 with SEC No. 136393 using the same antiquated Municipal Hall as its school building, the same edifice vacated by Trinidad Institute of Technology.
On October 23, 1986, the Municipality of Trinidad donated the land including the
 building and the improvements thereon, to TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE which then
 offered the following courses, namely:
 1. 2 years Diploma in Applied Technology major in Electricity and Construction
 2. 2 years Associate in Secretarial Administration
 3. 2 years Automotive Mechanic Course
 4. 1 year General Clerical Course
In 1993, a Bachelor of Arts degree was approved by the Department of Education, Culture and Sports (DECS).
After more than a decade of moderate existence, TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE faced a pressing risk of extinction. Wise enough in finding a solution, the Incorporators came up with a redemptive decision in a meeting to turn over, by way of a DEED OF SALE (CONFIRMATION OF TRANSFER OF OWNERHIP), the Ownership, Management and Control of TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE to the Local Government of Trinidad under the leadership of then Municipal Mayor, Engr. Filadelfo A. Garcia, Vice Mayor Francisco U. Gonzales and the Sangguniang Bayan Members during their term on August 1, 2003.
The sale by the Incorporators effectively transformed Trinidad Junior College into what is now known as the TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE (TMC), from 1997 up to present, one of the fastest growing Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Philippines.
Before Mayor Judith Del Rosario-Cajes assumed as the newly elected mayor of Trinidad on June 30, 2007, Trinidad Municipal College has a total enrolment of only 510 students. TMC became a recipeint of RA 10931 otherwise known as Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Act (UAQTEA) on the 2nd semester of academic year 2018-2019. Trinidad Municipal College was recognized by the Commission of Higher Education (CHED) as Higher Education Institution (HEI) pursuant to Commission en banc Resolution No. 564-2020 dated August 18, 2020.
At present, under the dynamic, strong and creative leadership of Former Mayor JUDITH DEL ROSARIO CAJES, wife of former congressman, and now Mayor ATTY. ROBERTO C. CAJES, Ph.D., TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE (TMC) quickly and steadily started to evolve physically and academically to meet the standards of a dynamically changing world of education and employment. As of 1st Semester, Academic Year 2022-2023, TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE has a total enrolment of more than 7,000 students and offers the following courses, to wit:

SECTION 1: INSTITUTIONAL OVERVIEW
1.1 History
Trinidad Municipal College (TMC) was established in 1980 through the vision of Mr. Paciano Petarco, with the support of Municipal Mayor Atty. Avelino N. Puracan. Originally named Trinidad Junior College, it was registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in 1985 with SEC No. 136393. On August 1, 2003, the ownership and management were turned over to the Local Government of Trinidad under the leadership of Mayor Engr. Filadelfo A. Garcia. The institution was renamed Trinidad Municipal College (TMC).
1.2 Legal Basis
- Republic Act 10931 (Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act, UAQTEA)
- CHED Memorandum Orders (CMOs) on programs and standards
- Local Government Code of the Philippines
- TMC Charter and By-Laws
1.3 Recognition
TMC was recognized by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) as a Higher Education Institution (HEI) pursuant to Commission en banc Resolution No. 564-2020 dated August 18, 2020.
1.4 Governance
TMC is an LGU-managed institution under the Municipality of Trinidad, Bohol. The College is headed by a College President who reports to the Municipal Mayor and the Sangguniang Bayan.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: VISION, MISSION, GOAL, PHILOSOPHY, AND SLOGAN
2.1 Vision
A model institution with fully developed academic, technical-vocational education and skill of manpower with positive work attitudes anchored in the core values of leadership and professionalism essential in the creation of self-reliant citizens.
2.2 Mission
To build well-trained, competent and employable professionals, who will meet the demands of the local and international work places.
2.3 Goal
TMC aims at evolving a whole individual as a child of God and a member of a democratic society who is professionally competent that can provide leadership and advance knowledge, well-trained in a certain vocation not only to help himself but to help others, and practical yet responsible and obedient to the laws of God and to the laws of the government.
2.4 Philosophy
TMC adheres to the philosophy that education is life and growth guided by faith in God and love of fellowmen in an environment of competitiveness, professionalism and excellence.
2.5 Slogan
TMC is committed to public educational services second to none.

SECTION 6: RETENTION AND ACADEMIC STANDING
6.1 Grading System
TMC uses the following grading system:
- 1.00 - 1.25: Excellent
- 1.26 - 1.50: Very Good
- 1.51 - 1.75: Good
- 1.76 - 2.00: Satisfactory
- 2.01 - 2.25: Fairly Satisfactory
- 2.26 - 2.50: Passing
- 2.51 - 3.00: Conditional
- 5.00: Failed
- INC: Incomplete
- DRP: Dropped
6.2 Retention Requirements
- Must maintain a General Weighted Average (GWA) of at least 2.00
- Must not have more than two (2) grades of 5.00 (Failed) in a semester
- Must not have been subjected to disciplinary action
- Must meet the specific retention requirements of the program
6.3 Academic Probation
- Students with a GWA below 2.00 are placed on academic probation
- Probationary students must improve their GWA within two (2) semesters
- Failure to improve results in academic dismissal
6.4 Academic Dismissal
- Students are academically dismissed if they:
 - Fail to improve their GWA after two (2) semesters of probation
 - Accumulate three (3) semesters of academic probation
 - Fail to meet the program's specific retention requirements
6.5 Re-admission After Dismissal
- Dismissed students may apply for re-admission after one (1) semester
- Re-admission is subject to approval by the Academic Affairs Office
- Re-admitted students are placed on probation for two (2) semesters

ACCOUNTING OFFICE MANUAL - TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE

Approved by: Office of the College President
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: OFFICE OVERVIEW
1.1 Mission
The Accounting Office of Trinidad Municipal College is committed to providing accurate, transparent, and timely financial services to support the academic and administrative operations of the institution.
1.2 Vision
To be a model accounting office that upholds the highest standards of financial integrity, accountability, and public service.
1.3 Functions
The Accounting Office is responsible for:
- Tuition and fee collection
- Financial record keeping and accounting
- Budget preparation and monitoring
- Financial reporting and auditing
- Disbursement of funds
- Payroll processing
- Procurement support
- CHED financial reporting
1.4 Staff Composition
- Accounting Head: Head of the office
- Staff Accountant: Handles general ledger and financial reporting
- Cashier: Manages collections and disbursements
- Bookkeeper: Maintains financial records
- Budget Officer: Prepares and monitors the budget
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: TUITION AND FEES
2.1 Tuition Fee Structure
TMC is an LGU-managed institution that offers free higher education under Republic Act 10931 (Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act, UAQTEA). The following applies:
- Full tuition fee waiver for all Filipino students enrolled in CHED-recognized programs under UAQTEA
- Miscellaneous fees may still apply as approved by the School Board
- Non-UNIFAST scholars pay the approved tuition fee rate
2.2 Miscellaneous Fees
The following miscellaneous fees are collected:
- Registration fee: PHP 200.00 per semester
- Library fee: PHP 150.00 per semester
- Laboratory fee (for laboratory courses): PHP 300.00 per semester
- Student activities fee: PHP 200.00 per semester
- Development fee: PHP 500.00 per semester
- Medical/dental fee: PHP 100.00 per semester
- Athletics fee: PHP 100.00 per semester
- Cultural fee: PHP 100.00 per semester
- School ID fee: PHP 100.00 (one-time)
- Insurance fee: PHP 100.00 per semester
2.3 Special Fees
- Late enrollment fee: PHP 200.00 (enrollment after the official period)
- Late payment fee: PHP 100.00 (payment after the due date)
- Change of schedule fee: PHP 50.00 per transaction
- Transcript of Records: PHP 50.00 per copy
- Certificate fee: PHP 30.00 per copy
- Graduation fee: PHP 800.00
- Diploma authentication: PHP 100.00 per copy
- Overtime fee (for special requests): PHP 50.00
2.4 Payment Schedule
- First installment: upon enrollment (at least 50% of total fees)
- Second installment: before midterms (remaining balance)
- Full payment option: upon enrollment (with 5% discount)
2.5 Mode of Payment
- Cash payment at the Accounting Office
- Bank deposit (for large amounts)
- Online payment (through the TMC payment portal)
- GCash and PayMaya (for selected fees)

SECTION 4: COLLECTION AND RECEIPTING
4.1 Official Receipts
- All payments must be acknowledged with an official receipt
- Official receipts are numbered and accounted for
- Duplicate receipts are issued only upon proper verification
- Forged or altered receipts are grounds for dismissal
4.2 Collection Procedures
- The Cashier receives all payments and issues official receipts
- Collections are deposited in the bank within twenty-four (24) hours
- Daily collection reports are prepared and submitted to the Accounting Head
- Monthly collection summaries are prepared for financial reporting
4.3 Acknowledgment Receipts
- For payments received outside the Accounting Office, an acknowledgment receipt is issued
- The acknowledgment receipt must be presented to the Accounting Office for official receipting
- Failure to convert an acknowledgment receipt to an official receipt within five (5) working days may result in forfeiture of the payment
4.4 Collections from External Sources
- Collections from grants, donations, and external projects must be deposited in the TMC official bank account
- A separate receipt must be issued for each collection
- Documentation must be maintained for audit purposes
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: DISBURSEMENT OF FUNDS
5.1 Disbursement Procedures
Step 1: Submit a disbursement request with supporting documents
Step 2: The Accounting Head reviews and approves the request
Step 3: The Budget Officer verifies budget availability
Step 4: The Cashier prepares the check or cash voucher
Step 5: The Accounting Head signs the disbursement voucher
Step 6: The College President or authorized signatory approves the payment
Step 7: The Cashier releases the payment
5.2 Disbursement Documents Required
- Approved purchase order or request
- Official receipt or invoice from the supplier
- Receiving report (for goods delivered)
- Certificate of completion (for services rendered)
- Payroll (for salaries and wages)
- Travel order and liquidation report (for travel expenses)
5.3 Check Signatories
- Checks below PHP 50,000.00: Accounting Head + Cashier
- Checks PHP 50,000.00 to PHP 200,000.00: Accounting Head + College President
- Checks above PHP 200,000.00: Accounting Head + College President + Budget Officer
5.4 Cash Disbursement
- Cash disbursements are limited to PHP 5,000.00 per transaction
- Cash disbursements require approval from the Accounting Head
- Petty cash is maintained for small day-to-day expenses
- Petty cash replenishment is done monthly
------------------------------------------------------------------------

OFFICE ADMINISTRATIVE MANUAL - TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE
Effective Date: Academic Year 2022-2023
Approved by: Office of the College President
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: INSTITUTIONAL OVERVIEW
1.1 Founding and History
Trinidad Municipal College (TMC) was founded in 1980 when Mr. Paciano Petarco initiated the plan for establishing a college institution in the Municipality of Trinidad, Bohol. The institution was originally named Trinidad Junior College and was registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in 1985 with SEC No. 136393. On August 1, 2003, the ownership and management were turned over to the Local Government of Trinidad. The institution was renamed Trinidad Municipal College (TMC).
1.2 Campus and Facilities
TMC's main campus is located in Trinidad, Bohol. The campus includes:
- Administration Building
- Academic buildings (multiple blocks)
- Library building
- Computer laboratory
- Science laboratory
- TESDA workshop area
- Auditorium and gymnasium
- Canteen and cafeteria
- Student lounge and study areas
- Parking area
- Open grounds and sports facilities
1.3 Organizational Structure
TMC is headed by a College President who is assisted by:
- Vice President for Academic Affairs
- Vice President for Administration and Finance
- College Registrar
- College Accounting Head
- HR Manager
- Director of Student Affairs
- Director of Extension and Community Relations
- Library Director
- Department Heads (per college)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: OFFICES AND THEIR FUNCTIONS
2.1 Office of the College President
- Provides overall leadership and direction for the institution
- Represents TMC in official functions and transactions
- Presides over the Academic Council and Administrative Council
- Approves policies, programs, and budgets
- Coordinates with the LGU, CHED, and other stakeholders
2.2 Office of the Vice President for Academic Affairs
- Supervises academic programs and instruction
- Coordinates curriculum development and review
- Monitors faculty performance and development
- Oversees academic calendar implementation
- Manages academic quality assurance
2.3 Office of the Vice President for Administration and Finance
- Supervises administrative and financial operations
- Manages budget preparation and utilization
- Coordinates procurement and logistics
- Oversees human resource management
- Ensures compliance with financial regulations
2.4 Registrar's Office
- Manages student enrollment and registration
- Maintains student academic records
- Issues official academic documents
- Coordinates graduation and credentialing
- Ensures CHED compliance and reporting
2.5 Accounting Office
- Manages tuition and fee collection
- Maintains financial records and accounting
- Prepares financial reports
- Processes disbursements and payroll
- Coordinates budget monitoring
2.6 Human Resource Management Office
- Manages recruitment and selection
- Handles employee records and benefits
- Coordinates training and development
- Implements performance management
- Ensures labor relations compliance
2.7 Student Affairs Office
- Manages student organizations and activities
- Coordinates student discipline and welfare
- Provides guidance and counseling services
- Manages scholarship programs
- Oversees student development programs
2.8 Library
- Provides access to print and digital resources
- Supports research and learning
- Manages library operations and services
- Coordinates information literacy programs
- Maintains library collection
2.9 Extension and Community Relations Office
- Coordinates community extension programs
- Manages partnerships with external organizations
- Implements outreach and social responsibility programs
- Coordinates extension services
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: OFFICE HOURS AND OPERATIONS
3.1 Regular Office Hours
- Monday to Friday: 7:30 AM to 5:00 PM
- Saturday: 7:30 AM to 12:00 PM (for selected services)
- Sunday and Holidays: Closed
3.2 Transaction Hours
- Student transactions: 7:30 AM to 4:30 PM
- Internal transactions: 7:30 AM to 5:00 PM
- Cashier operations: 7:30 AM to 4:00 PM
3.3 Emergency Procedures
- Fire: proceed to the nearest exit and assemble at the designated evacuation area
- Earthquake: duck, cover, and hold; proceed to open area after shaking stops
- Medical emergency: call for first aid assistance and proceed to the health office
- Security incident: report to the nearest security personnel and follow evacuation procedures
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: DOCUMENT MANAGEMENT
4.1 Incoming Documents
- All incoming documents are received by the Records Section
- Documents are logged in the incoming document registry
- Documents are routed to the appropriate office within three (3) working days
- Action taken on documents is recorded in the registry
4.2 Outgoing Documents
- All outgoing documents are prepared by the originating office
- Documents must be reviewed and approved by the appropriate authority
- Documents are logged in the outgoing document registry
- Documents are dispatched through the Records Section
4.3 Official Communications
- Official communications must use the TMC letterhead
- Communications must be signed by the appropriate authority
- Digital communications must use the official TMC email address
- Social media communications must follow the TMC Social Media Policy
4.4 Records Retention
- Administrative records: seven (7) years
- Financial records: seven (7) years
- Academic records: permanently
- Legal records: as required by law
- Transitory records: three (3) years
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: PROCUREMENT AND LOGISTICS
5.1 Procurement Process
Step 1: Requisition is submitted by the requesting office
Step 2: Budget availability is verified by the Accounting Office
Step 3: Procurement is processed by the Supply and Property Office
Step 4: Bidding or negotiation is conducted as required
Step 5: Award is made to the winning bidder
Step 6: Goods or services are delivered and inspected
Step 7: Payment is processed by the Accounting Office
5.2 Procurement Methods
- Small value procurement: below PHP 50,000.00
- Competitive bidding: PHP 50,000.00 and above
- Alternative methods: as allowed by RA 9184 (Government Procurement Reform Act)
5.3 Property Management
- All TMC property is inventoried and tagged
- Property disposal follows government property disposal rules
- Annual physical inventory is conducted
- Property records are maintained by the Supply and Property Office
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: INFORMATION AND COMMUNICATION TECHNOLOGY
6.1 Computer and Internet Use
- TMC provides computer and internet access for academic purposes
- Personal use is limited to break times
- Downloading of unauthorized software is prohibited
- Sharing of login credentials is prohibited
6.2 Data Privacy
- All employees must comply with the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173)
- Personal information must be collected and processed lawfully
- Data breaches must be reported immediately to the Data Protection Officer
6.3 Social Media Policy
- Official TMC social media accounts are managed by the designated office
- Employees must not post confidential information on social media
- Social media must not be used for personal political advocacy
- Violations may result in disciplinary action
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7: SAFETY AND SECURITY

7.2 Fire Prevention
- Fire exits are clearly marked and must be kept clear
- Fire extinguishers are placed in designated areas
- Fire drills are conducted at least twice a year
- Open flames and smoking are prohibited inside buildings
7.3 Emergency Contact Numbers
- Campus Security: (038) XXX-XXXX

- Hospital: (038) XXX-XXXX

SECTION 8: CAMPUS RULES AND REGULATIONS
8.1 Student Conduct
- Students must wear proper identification at all times on campus
- Students must dress appropriately (no slippers, tank tops, or revealing clothing inside classrooms)
- Students must maintain order and decorum in all campus areas

8.2 Prohibited Items

8.3 Visitor Policy

9.1 TMC Quality Policy
TMC is committed to providing quality education and services through continuous improvement, innovation, and stakeholder satisfaction.
9.2 Quality Objectives

9.3 Continuous Improvement

FACULTY MANUAL - TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE
Effective Date: Academic Year 2022-2023
Approved by: Office of the College President
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS
1.1 Purpose
This Faculty Manual establishes the policies, guidelines, and procedures governing the academic and professional conduct of all faculty members at Trinidad Municipal College (TMC). It serves as a reference for faculty rights, responsibilities, and performance standards.
1.2 Scope
This manual applies to all full-time, part-time, and adjunct faculty members across all colleges and departments of TMC, including the College of Teacher Education, College of Arts and Sciences, College of Computer Studies, College of Criminal Justice Education, and College of Office Administration.
1.3 Vision
TMC faculty are committed to being catalysts for academic excellence, producing graduates who are competent, competitive, and socially responsible professionals.
1.4 Mission
The faculty of TMC shall provide quality instruction, conduct meaningful research, and extend community service to produce well-rounded graduates who can contribute to local and national development.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: FACULTY QUALIFICATIONS
2.1 Minimum Qualifications
All faculty members must meet the following minimum qualifications:
- Bachelor's degree in the relevant field for instructor positions
- Master's degree in the relevant field for senior instructor positions
- Doctoral degree for professorial positions
- Must be a Filipino citizen or a foreign national with proper work authorization
- Must possess good moral character and no criminal record
- Must pass the TMC faculty selection process
2.2 Required Documents
Applicants must submit the following during application:
- Updated resume or curriculum vitae
- Transcript of records and diploma (original or certified true copy)
- Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) license, if applicable
- National Computer Center (NCC) certification, for IT faculty
- Certificate of employment from previous employers
- NBI or police clearance
- Medical certificate
- Two (2) character reference letters
2.3 Preferred Qualifications
- Teaching experience in higher education
- Industry experience relevant to the program being taught
- Published research papers or presentations
- Membership in professional organizations
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: ACADEMIC RANKS AND PROMOTIONS
3.1 Faculty Ranks
TMC recognizes the following academic ranks:
- Instructor: Minimum of a Bachelor's degree with relevant experience
- Senior Instructor: Master's degree with at least 3 years teaching experience
- Assistant Professor: Doctoral degree or Master's degree with significant publications
- Associate Professor: Doctoral degree with extensive publications and service
- Professor: Doctoral degree with outstanding contributions to education and research
3.2 Promotion Criteria
Promotion is based on the following criteria:
- Academic qualifications and advanced degrees
- Years of teaching experience at TMC
- Research productivity (publications, presentations)
- Community extension involvement
- Student evaluation ratings
- Administrative performance, if applicable
- Professional development activities
3.3 Promotion Process
Faculty members seeking promotion must:
1. Submit a letter of intent to the Department Head
2. Prepare a promotion portfolio with supporting documents
3. Submit the portfolio to the Academic Affairs Office
4. Undergo evaluation by the Promotion Committee
5. Present before the Academic Council
6. Await approval from the College President and CHED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: TEACHING LOAD AND SCHEDULING
4.1 Standard Teaching Load
- Full-time faculty: minimum of 15 units, maximum of 24 units per semester
- Part-time faculty: maximum of 12 units per semester
- overload teaching requires approval from the Department Head and Academic Affairs
4.2 Class Schedule
- Morning classes: 7:00 AM to 12:00 PM
- Afternoon classes: 1:00 PM to 5:00 PM
- Evening classes: 5:30 PM to 9:00 PM (for working students)
- Saturday classes: 7:00 AM to 12:00 PM (for TVET programs)
4.3 Faculty Availability
- Full-time faculty must be available for at least 8 hours per day on campus
- Faculty must hold regular office hours for student consultation: at least 2 hours per week
- Faculty must be reachable through official TMC communication channels
4.4 Load Distribution
The Department Head shall distribute the teaching load equitably among faculty members, considering:
- Faculty specialization and expertise
- Student enrollment demand
- Program accreditation requirements
- Faculty preference and availability
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: CURRICULUM AND INSTRUCTION
5.1 Curriculum Implementation
- Faculty must follow the CHED-approved curriculum for their program
- Any curriculum modification must go through the Academic Council
- Course syllabi must be submitted to the Department Head before the start of each semester
- Syllabi must include learning objectives, course content, teaching methodology, and assessment criteria
5.2 Course Syllabus Requirements
Each course syllabus must contain:
- Course description and rationale
- Course objectives and learning outcomes
- Weekly schedule of topics
- Required and recommended readings
- Grading system and criteria
- Attendance policy
- Academic integrity policy
- Reference list
5.3 Teaching Methodology
Faculty are encouraged to use varied teaching methods including:
- Lecture-discussion
- Problem-based learning
- Cooperative learning
- Computer-assisted instruction
- Field trips and industry visits
- Capstone projects and research
- Service learning
5.4 Assessment and Grading
- Faculty must follow the TMC Grading System as approved by the Academic Council
- Grades must be submitted within five (5) working days after the examination period
- Faculty must maintain grade records and submit them to the Registrar
- Any grade change must be approved by the Department Head and Academic Affairs
5.5 Attendance Monitoring
- Faculty must submit attendance records daily through the faculty portal
- Students exceeding 20% absences in a course must be reported to the Academic Affairs Office
- Faculty must submit a mid-term grade for students at risk of failing
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: FACULTY EVALUATION
6.1 Student Evaluation of Faculty
- Students shall evaluate their instructors at the end of each semester
- Evaluation shall be conducted through the TMC Faculty Evaluation Form
- Results shall be compiled by the Academic Affairs Office
- Individual results shall be shared with the faculty member and Department Head
6.2 Peer Evaluation
- Faculty members shall undergo peer evaluation at least once per academic year
- Peer evaluation shall be conducted by a colleague from the same department
- The evaluation shall focus on teaching effectiveness, classroom management, and professional engagement
6.3 Administrative Evaluation
- The Department Head shall evaluate each faculty member at least once per semester
- The evaluation shall consider teaching performance, research, extension, and service
- Results shall be used for promotion, tenure, and professional development planning
6.4 Performance Rating
Faculty performance shall be rated as follows:
- Outstanding: consistently exceeds expectations in all areas
- Very Satisfactory: exceeds expectations in most areas
- Satisfactory: meets expectations in all areas
- Needs Improvement: below expectations in some areas
- Unsatisfactory: below expectations in most areas
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7: RESEARCH AND PUBLICATION
7.1 Research Encouragement
TMC encourages all faculty members to engage in research activities related to their field of expertise. Research is an integral component of the faculty evaluation and promotion system.
7.2 Research Requirements
- Full-time faculty must participate in at least one (1) research project per academic year
- Faculty must present their research findings at least once per academic year
- Research output may include journal articles, conference papers, book chapters, or patents
7.3 Research Ethics
- All research involving human subjects must be approved by the TMC Research Ethics Committee
- Research must comply with the National Ethics Guidelines for Health and Health-Related Research
- Plagiarism and fabrication of data are strictly prohibited
7.4 Research Support
- TMC provides research grants for approved research proposals
- Faculty may request leave for research-related activities
- Research publication fees may be subsidized by TMC, subject to budget availability
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8: PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
8.1 Training and Development
TMC supports the continuous professional development of its faculty through:
- In-service training programs
- Seminars and workshops
- Conferences and symposia
- Graduate studies and advanced training
- Industry immersion programs
8.2 Study Leave
- Faculty with at least five (5) years of service may apply for study leave
- Study leave shall not exceed one (1) academic year
- The faculty member must return to TMC for at least two (2) years after study leave
- Study leave may be granted with or without pay, depending on available budget
8.3 Continuing Education
- Faculty are encouraged to pursue graduate studies
- TMC may provide tuition subsidy for faculty pursuing master's or doctoral degrees
- Faculty must maintain a grade of at least 85% to qualify for tuition subsidy
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9: CODE OF CONDUCT
9.1 Professional Ethics
Faculty members shall:
- Conduct themselves professionally at all times
- Maintain the highest standards of academic integrity
- Treat students, colleagues, and staff with respect and fairness
- Avoid conflicts of interest
- Protect confidential information
9.2 Prohibited Conduct
Faculty members shall NOT:
- Engage in any form of sexual harassment or discrimination
- Accept gifts or favors that may influence academic decisions
- Use TMC resources for personal gain
- Engage in political activities during working hours
- Publicly criticize TMC policies or officials without following proper channels
- Fail to report academic dishonesty or misconduct
9.3 Disciplinary Actions
Violations of the Code of Conduct may result in:
- Verbal or written warning
- Suspension without pay
- Demotion
- Dismissal or termination of employment
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 10: LEAVES AND ABSENCES
10.1 Leave Entitlements
- Vacation leave: fifteen (15) working days per academic year
- Sick leave: fifteen (15) working days per academic year
- Maternity leave: as provided by law (Republic Act 11210)
- Paternity leave: as provided by law (Republic Act 8187)
- Solo parent leave: as provided by law (Republic Act 8972)
- Special leave for women: as provided by law (Republic Act 9710)
10.2 Leave Application
- Faculty must submit a leave application form at least three (3) working days before the intended leave date
- Emergency leaves must be reported to the Department Head as soon as possible
- Leaves exceeding five (5) consecutive working days require approval from the Academic Affairs Office
10.3 Absent Without Leave (AWOL)
- Faculty who are absent without approved leave for three (3) consecutive working days shall be deemed to have abandoned their position
- A show-cause memo shall be issued before any disciplinary action is taken
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 11: BENEFITS AND COMPENSATION
11.1 Salary
- Faculty salaries are based on the TMC Salary Standardization Table
- Salary grades are determined by academic rank and qualifications
- Annual salary increments are subject to performance evaluation
11.2 Benefits
TMC provides the following benefits to its faculty members:
- GSIS (Government Service Insurance System) contributions
- PhilHealth contributions
- Pag-IBIG Fund contributions
- 13th month pay
- Rice subsidy
- Medical allowance
- Book allowance
- Uniform allowance (for new hires)
11.3 Retirement
- Faculty members retire at age 65 or upon completion of 15 years of service, whichever comes later
- Retirement benefits are in accordance with GSIS and TMC policies
- A retirement ceremony shall be held in honor of retiring faculty members
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 12: GRIEVANCE PROCEDURE
12.1 Filing a Grievance
- Faculty members who feel aggrieved may file a written complaint with the Department Head
- If the grievance is not resolved at the department level, it may be elevated to the Academic Affairs Office
- The grievance must be filed within thirty (30) working days from the date of the incident
12.2 Grievance Committee
- A Grievance Committee shall be composed of three (3) members: one from the faculty, one from the administration, and one from the HR office
- The committee shall hear both parties and render a decision within fifteen (15) working days
12.3 Appeal
- Either party may appeal the decision of the Grievance Committee to the College President
- The College President's decision is final and binding
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 13: AMENDMENTS
This Faculty Manual may be amended or revised as needed by the Academic Council, subject to approval by the College President. Amendments shall take effect upon publication and dissemination to all faculty members.

HR MANUAL - TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE
Effective Date: Academic Year 2022-2023
Approved by: Office of the College President
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: OFFICE OVERVIEW
1.1 Mission
The Human Resource Management Office (HRMO) of Trinidad Municipal College is committed to attracting, developing, and retaining a competent workforce to support the institution's mission of providing quality education.
1.2 Vision
To be a strategic HR partner that fosters a positive work environment, promotes employee welfare, and ensures organizational excellence.
1.3 Functions
The HRMO is responsible for:
- Recruitment and selection of personnel
- Onboarding and orientation
- Performance management
- Employee relations and welfare
- Compensation and benefits administration
- Training and development
- Records management
- Labor relations and compliance
1.4 Staff Composition
- HR Manager: Head of the office
- HR Officer: Handles recruitment, employee relations, and benefits
- HR Assistant: Administrative support and records management
- Payroll Officer: Manages salary and benefits processing
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: RECRUITMENT AND SELECTION
2.1 Recruitment Process
Step 1: Position is declared vacant by the Department Head
Step 2: HRMO posts the vacancy announcement (internal and external)
Step 3: Applications are received and screened
Step 4: Qualified applicants are shortlisted
Step 5: Interview is conducted by the Selection Committee
Step 6: Written exam or skills test (if applicable)
Step 7: Final interview with the College President
Step 8: Selection and appointment
2.2 Required Documents for Application
- Application letter addressed to the HR Manager
- Updated resume or curriculum vitae
- Transcript of records and diploma
- Professional license or certification (if applicable)
- Certificate of employment from previous employers
- NBI or police clearance
- Medical certificate
- Two (2) references with contact information
- Portfolio of work (for applicable positions)
2.3 Selection Criteria
Candidates are evaluated based on:
- Educational background and qualifications
- Relevant experience and skills
- Interview performance
- Written exam results (if applicable)
- Character references
- Alignment with TMC's vision and values
2.4 Appointment Types
- Permanent appointment: for regular positions with indefinite tenure
- Contract of Service (COS): for temporary or project-based positions
- Job Order: for short-term assignments not exceeding six (6) months
- Part-time appointment: for positions requiring less than full-time service
2.5 Probationary Period
- New employees undergo a probationary period of six (6) months
- Performance is evaluated during the probationary period
- Successful completion leads to permanent appointment
- Unsatisfactory performance may result in non-renewal of appointment
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: COMPENSATION AND BENEFITS
3.1 Salary Standardization
- TMC follows the Salary Standardization Table (SST) for LGU employees
- Salary grades are determined by position and qualifications
- Annual salary increases are provided through the SSEF (Salary Standardization and Enhancement Fund)
3.2 Mandatory Benefits
All employees are entitled to:
- GSIS (Government Service Insurance System) contributions
- PhilHealth (Philippine Health Insurance Corporation) contributions
- Pag-IBIG Fund (Home Development Mutual Fund) contributions
- 13th month pay (released in December)
- Service Incentive Leave (SIL): five (5) days per year
3.3 Additional Benefits
- Rice subsidy: PHP 1,500.00 per month
- Medical allowance: PHP 5,000.00 per year
- Book allowance: PHP 2,000.00 per year
- Uniform allowance: PHP 3,000.00 (for new hires)
- Laundry allowance: PHP 200.00 per month (for designated positions)
- Transportation allowance: as approved by the College President
3.4 Leave Benefits
- Vacation leave: fifteen (15) working days per year
- Sick leave: fifteen (15) working days per year
- Maternity leave: one hundred five (105) days (RA 11210)
- Paternity leave: seven (7) days (RA 8187)
- Solo parent leave: sixty (60) days (RA 8972)
- Special leave for women: sixty (60) days (RA 9710)
- study leave: as approved by the College President
- Mandatory continuing leave: as per civil service rules
3.5 Leave Application
Step 1: Fill out the Leave Application Form
Step 2: Submit to the immediate supervisor for endorsement
Step 3: The HRMO reviews and processes the leave
Step 4: The College President approves leaves exceeding five (5) days
Step 5: The approved leave form is returned to the HRMO for record
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT
4.1 Performance Evaluation System
TMC implements the Results-Based Performance Management System (RPMS) for all employees.
4.2 Evaluation Period
- Mid-year evaluation: January to June
- End-year evaluation: July to December
- Employees with less than six (6) months of service are not evaluated
4.3 Performance Indicators
Employees are evaluated on the following Key Result Areas (KRAs):
- Core competencies (values, behaviors)
- Technical competencies (job-specific skills)
- Leadership competencies (for supervisory positions)
4.4 Rating Scale
- Outstanding: consistently exceeds expectations (5)
- Very Satisfactory: exceeds expectations (4)
- Satisfactory: meets expectations (3)
- Needs Improvement: below expectations (2)
- Unsatisfactory: far below expectations (1)
4.5 Performance Incentives
- Performance-Based Bonus (PBB): as approved by the DBM
- Productivity Incentive: as approved by the School Board
- Longevity Pay: PHP 2,000.00 per year of service
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: TRAINING AND DEVELOPMENT
5.1 Training Programs
TMC provides the following training opportunities:
- Orientation for new employees
- Skills development training
- Leadership and management training
- Technical training
- Mandatory seminars (gender sensitivity, anti-sexual harassment, data privacy)
5.2 Training Nomination
- Department Heads nominate employees for training based on performance and development needs
- The HRMO consolidates training nominations and prepares the training plan
- Training budget is allocated annually
5.3 Study Leave
- Employees with at least five (5) years of service may apply for study leave
- Study leave may be granted with or without pay
- Employees must return to TMC for at least two (2) years after study leave
5.4 Continuing Professional Education (CPE)
- TMC supports employees in pursuing CPE requirements
- CPE credits may be earned through seminars, workshops, and conferences
- CPE fees may be subsidized by TMC, subject to budget availability
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: EMPLOYEE CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE
6.1 Code of Conduct
All employees must:
- Perform their duties with diligence and professionalism
- Treat colleagues, students, and stakeholders with respect
- Maintain the confidentiality of sensitive information
- Comply with all TMC policies and government regulations
- Report any violations of the Code of Conduct
6.2 Prohibited Conduct
Employees shall NOT:
- Engage in any form of harassment or discrimination
- Accept gifts or favors that may influence official actions
- Use TMC resources for personal gain
- Engage in outside employment without proper authorization
- Be absent without approved leave (AWOL)
- Fail to report violations or misconduct
6.3 Disciplinary Actions
Violations may result in:
- Verbal warning (first offense)
- Written warning (second offense)
- Suspension without pay (third offense)
- Demotion (repeated offenses)
- Dismissal or termination (grave offenses)
6.4 Due Process
All disciplinary actions follow the due process requirement:
Step 1: Issuance of a show-cause memo
Step 2: Employee submits a written explanation within five (5) working days
Step 3: Administrative hearing is conducted
Step 4: Decision is rendered by the Disciplinary Committee
Step 5: Appeal may be made to the College President within fifteen (15) working days
Step 6: The College President's decision is final
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7: SEPARATION AND RETIREMENT
7.1 Resignation
- Employees must submit a resignation letter at least thirty (30) days before the intended date
- Clearance from all offices must be completed before separation
- Final pay is released within thirty (30) days after separation
7.2 Retirement
- Regular retirement age: sixty (60) years old
- Compulsory retirement age: sixty-five (65) years old
- Early retirement: as provided by law and TMC policies
- Retirement benefits include:
 - Retirement pay (as per GSIS or TMC policy)
 - Pro-rated 13th month pay
 - Cash equivalent of unused leave credits
 - Retirement ceremony
7.3 End of Contract
- Contract of Service employees are separated at the end of their contract
- Performance evaluation may be conducted before contract renewal
- Separation pay is not applicable for COS employees
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8: EMPLOYEE WELFARE
8.1 Health and Safety
- TMC provides a safe and healthy work environment
- Regular health and safety inspections are conducted
- First aid kits are available in all offices and classrooms
- Emergency evacuation plans are in place
8.2 Employee Assistance
- Counseling services are available for employees in need
- Financial literacy programs are conducted annually
- Family day and recreational activities are organized
8.3 Grievance Mechanism
- Employees may file grievances through the HRMO
- The Grievance Committee investigates and resolves disputes
- Confidentiality is maintained throughout the process
- Retaliation against complainants is strictly prohibited
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9: RECORDS MANAGEMENT
9.1 Employee Records
The HRMO maintains the following records for each employee:
- Personal information sheet
- Employment contract or appointment paper
- Performance evaluation records
- Training and development records
- Leave records
- Disciplinary records
- Salary and benefits records
9.2 Record Retention
- Employee records are retained for the duration of employment plus ten (10) years
- Disciplinary records are retained for five (5) years after the final action

SECTION 1: RESEARCH OVERVIEW
1.1 Purpose
This Research Manual establishes the policies, guidelines, and procedures for conducting research at Trinidad Municipal College (TMC). It ensures that research activities are conducted ethically, rigorously, and in compliance with national and institutional standards.
1.2 Research Philosophy
TMC believes that research is essential for institutional growth, faculty development, and student learning. Research activities should contribute to the advancement of knowledge, address community needs, and support the college's mission.
1.3 Research Objectives
- Promote a culture of research among faculty and students
- Generate new knowledge relevant to the local community and beyond
- Improve teaching and learning through evidence-based practices
- Contribute to national development through research and innovation
- Establish partnerships with other institutions and organizations
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: RESEARCH ETHICS
2.1 Ethical Principles
All research at TMC must adhere to the following ethical principles:
- Respect for persons: participants must be treated with dignity and respect
- Beneficence: research must maximize benefits and minimize harm
- Justice: research must be fair and equitable
- Informed consent: participants must voluntarily agree to participate
- Confidentiality: participant information must be kept confidential
- Integrity: research must be conducted with honesty and transparency
2.2 Research Ethics Committee (REC)
- The REC is responsible for reviewing and approving all research involving human participants
- The REC is composed of five (5) members: chairperson, two (2) faculty representatives, one (2) external member, and one (2) community representative
- The REC meets at least once a month
- Research proposals must be submitted to the REC at least two (2) weeks before the scheduled meeting
2.3 Ethical Review Process
Step 1: Researcher submits the research proposal to the REC
Step 2: REC reviews the proposal for ethical compliance
Step 3: REC provides feedback or requests revisions
Step 4: Researcher revises the proposal as needed
Step 5: REC approves the proposal with an ethics clearance certificate
Step 6: Researcher conducts the research with ongoing ethical oversight
2.4 Informed Consent
- Participants must be provided with a consent form that includes:
 - Purpose and procedures of the research
 - Expected benefits and risks
 - Right to withdraw at any time
 - Confidentiality measures
 - Contact information of the researcher and REC
- Participants must sign the consent form before participating
- For minors, parental consent and minor assent are required
2.5 Risk Assessment
- Research involving vulnerable populations requires additional ethical review
- Research involving deception must be justified and must not cause harm
- Research involving sensitive topics requires additional confidentiality measures
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: RESEARCH PROPOSAL
3.1 Proposal Requirements
A research proposal must include the following:
- Title of the study
- Statement of the problem
- Review of related literature
- Theoretical or conceptual framework
- Research methodology
- Ethical considerations
- Timeline
- Budget
- References
3.2 Proposal Format
Research proposals must follow the prescribed format:
- Font: Times New Roman, 12pt
- Spacing: double-spaced
- Margins: 1 inch on all sides
- Citation style: APA (American Psychological Association) 7th Edition
- Page numbers: bottom center
3.3 Proposal Submission
- Faculty research proposals must be endorsed by the Department Head
- Student research proposals must be endorsed by the research adviser
- Proposals must be submitted to the REC through the Research Office
- Electronic copies must be submitted through the TMC Research Portal
3.4 Proposal Evaluation
Proposals are evaluated based on:
- Significance and relevance of the study
- Soundness of the research design and methodology
- Feasibility and timeliness
- Ethical compliance
- Qualifications of the research team
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
4.1 Quantitative Research
- Experimental, quasi-experimental, and non-experimental designs
- Surveys and questionnaires
- Statistical analysis (descriptive and inferential)
- Validity and reliability of instruments
4.2 Qualitative Research
- Case study, phenomenological, grounded theory, and ethnographic designs
- Interviews, focus group discussions, and observations
- Thematic analysis and coding
- Trustworthiness criteria (credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability)
4.3 Mixed Methods Research
- Sequential, concurrent, and transformative designs
- Integration of quantitative and qualitative approaches
- Triangulation of data sources
4.4 Action Research
- Conducted by faculty to improve teaching and learning
- Cyclical process: plan, act, observe, reflect
- Results are used for program improvement
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: STUDENT RESEARCH
5.1 Undergraduate Research
- All programs require a capstone project or thesis
- Research courses are integrated into the curriculum
- Students must enroll in the research course during their final year
5.2 Thesis Requirements
- Must be original and not previously submitted for another degree
- Must follow the TMC thesis format and guidelines
- Must be defended before a panel of evaluators
- Must be submitted to the library and Research Office
5.3 Research Adviser
- Each student researcher is assigned a research adviser
- The adviser guides the student through the research process
- The adviser must have expertise in the research topic
- The adviser must be available for consultation and mentoring
5.4 Research Panel
- The panel consists of three (3) to five (5) members
- The panel includes the research adviser and external evaluators
- The panel evaluates the research proposal and final defense
- The panel provides feedback and recommendations
5.5 Plagiarism Policy
- Plagiarism is strictly prohibited at TMC
- All research papers are checked for plagiarism using Turnitin or similar software
- Plagiarism may result in:
 - Automatic failure of the research course
 - Disciplinary action
 - Revocation of degree (if discovered after graduation)

Brief history of the college 
 
The Vision to establish an affordable College in the Municipality of Trinidad was not 
realized overnight. It took many years of trials, hardships and collective efforts to bring 
forth what is now popularly known as TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE, one of the fastest 
growing Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Philippines. 
 
It started in 1980 when the Municipal Officials of Trinidad, Bohol talked to a Davao-
based educator and businessman into putting up a branch of his private vocational school in 
Trinidad. The lone antiquated building in Trinidad, Bohol, used and inaugurated in 1957 as 
the original Municipal Hall of Trinidad was then converted into this private school named as 
Trinidad Institute of Technology (TIT). TIT offered short-term vocational courses, such as 
Typewriting, Stenography, Tailoring and Bookkeeping. 
 
Trinidad Institute of Technology (TIT) did not last longer than a semester as it “died a 
natural death”, so to speak. During this time the vision to establish a College in Trinidad 
became vague. It was in 1985 that the vision to re-establish a college that would offer 
affordable yet quality education to students from lower income families not only from 
Trinidad but also from the neighboring towns, did not go in vain. That same year, a group of 
Trinidadnons formed themselves as Incorporators and registered the TRINIDAD JUNIOR 
COLLEGE with the Securities and Exchange Commission with SEC No. 136393, using the 
same antiquated Municipal Hall as its school building, the same edifice vacated by Trinidad 
Institute of Technology. 
 
On October 23, 1986, the Municipality of Trinidad donated the land including the 
building and the improvements thereon, to TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE which then offered 
the following courses, namely: 
 
 2 years Diploma in Applied Technology major in Electricity and Construction 
 2 years Associate in Secretarial Administration 
 2 years Automotive Mechanic Course 
 1 year General Clerical Course 
 
In 1993, a Bachelor of Arts degree was approved by the Department of Education, 
Culture and Sports (DECS). 
 
After more than a decade of moderate existence, TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE faced 
a pressing risk of extinction. Wise enough in finding a solution, the Incorporators
[Page 4]

came up with a redemptive decision in a meeting to turn over, by way of a DEED OF SALE 
(CONFIRMATION OF TRANSFER OF OWNERHIP), the Ownership, Management and Control 
of TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE to the Local Government of Trinidad on August 1, 2003. The 
sale by the Incorporators effectively transformed Trinidad Junior College into what is now 
known as the TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE (TMC), one of the fastest growing Higher 
Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Philippines. 
 
Before Mayor Judith Del Rosario-Cajes assumed as the newly elected mayor of

SECTION 1. COLLEGE PRESIDENT 
 
The administration of the College shall be vested in the College President who shall render 
full time service. The powers and duties of the college president shall perform specific 
powers and duties as follows: 
 
1. He/She shall have general supervision and control of all business matters as well as 
all administrative and financial operations of TMC; 
2. He/She shall be ex-officio head of all officers, members of the teaching 
staff, administrative and all other employees of TMC; 
3. He/She shall carry on the general policies lay down by the BOT and shall have the 
power to act within the limits of the said general policies. 
4. He/She shall have the power to determine and prepare agenda of all meetings of the 
BOT, the Administrative Council and of the Academic Council; provided, however, 
that any member shall have the right to suggest any matter for inclusion in the 
agenda; 
5. He/She shall preside at commencement exercises and other public affairs of the 
College and confer such degrees and honors as confirmed by the BOT. All diplomas 
and certificates issued by the institution shall be signed by him/her by the 
College/College Registrar; 
6. He/She shall be the official link of communication between the faculty, personnel, 
and students of the institution on the one hand, and the BOT or the Board of 
Trustees on the other; 
7. He/She can appoint competent and qualified persons to fill all vacancies and new 
positions as delegated by the BOT. He/She shall have the authority to fill vacancies 
temporarily and to make such arrangements as necessary to meet all exigencies that 
may occur between the meetings of the BOT so as not to unduly hamper the 
operations of the institution; 
8. He/She shall have the authority to transfer faculty members and employees from 
one department or unit of the institution to another in accordance with existing laws 
and rules subject to the confirmation by the BOT; 
9. He/She shall hold all officers, faculty and employees of the institution, duty-bound to 
the full discharge of their respective duties. When necessity arises and in the interest 
of public service, he/she shall after consultation with the Dean or the Head of Office 
or Unit concerned, initiate the necessary proceedings for the separation from the 
service of the erring faculty members or administrative personnel; 
10. He/She shall execute and sign, on behalf of the institution all contracts, deeds and all 
such other instruments necessary for the proper conduct of business of the 
institution. However, in recurring undertakings and transactions where the action is 
virtually ministerial, and the conditions and terms for which have been fixed in 
accordance with the existing regulations of the institution and general laws of the 
land, the specified cases may be made in his/her behalf, by the officers of the

ARTICLE Ii 
Academic policies 
 
SECTION 1. ADMISSION POLICY 
 
The school adopts a policy of open admission to a student with complete necessary 
credentials upon enrollment and whose qualification is subject to other prescribed 
admission requirements. 
 
1.1 
The basic requirement for eligibility for admission of a student to any course in the 
College shall be graduation from a Senior High School from Department of Education 
(DepEd) recognized Secondary Schools. 
 
Pursuant RA 10931, CHED-Unifast Memorandum order and to prevailing Law/Policy 
on Alternative Learning System-Accreditation and Equivalency Program (ALS-A&E), 
TMC recognizes its benefits regarding enrollment of ALS graduates ending 2nd 
semester AY 2020. 
 
However, ALS graduates are eligible for enrolment in the non-board examination 
courses only. 
 
1.2 
Admission to Free Education is open to all students who pass the college entrance 
examination either board and non-board courses and meet the school’s specific 
standards, requirements and regulations as provided in this manual. 
 
Continuing students who passed a non-board entrance examination and wish to shift 
to a board course must undergo the admission test for board courses prior to their 
admission. 
 
For admission of non-passers, they will have to execute voluntary waivers in order to 
be enrolled at TMC and pay their required tuition and other school fees. 
 
1.3 
The student who qualifies for enrollment shall stay for the entire period in which 
he/she is expected to complete his/her course without prejudice to his/her right to 
transfer to other schools within the prescribed period provided that said student has 
meet the retention policies as provided in this manual. 
 
1.4 
No student is deemed officially enrolled unless he/she submits the required 
admission documents upon enrollment. 
 
1.5 
A student is deemed officially enrolled after he/she has been given a printed Study 
Load by the School Registrar. 
 
The releasing of Study Load to students will be done after the Final Subject Loading 
of Instructor.
[Page 18]

A student is eligible for admission to board courses provided he/she meets the 
following: 
 
a. Must be a Senior High School Graduate; 
 
a.1 Those applicants with partial grade must comply with grade requirement per 
program upon enrolment; and 
a.2 Those who failed to comply the grade requirements of a.1 mentioned-above 
are advised to enroll to non-board courses.

6.2 
INCOMPLETE (INC). INC is given in cases where the students did not take the final 
examination in any subject and his/her overall performance during the Prelim, 
Midterm and Semi-final taken together would not be sufficient to merit a final 
passing grade. An INC does not earn credit but does not indicate failure either. Those 
with INC must file a Completion Form within a period of one (1) semester reckoned 
from the semester the INC was incurred in order to be granted a final passing grade 
otherwise failure to do so means an automatic failing grade of 5.0. In every school 
term, the School Registrar sets a schedule for the filing of application to allow 
students to comply with this rule, without interrupting the usual function/operation 
of the office. 
 
INC shall be given to student who failed to take examinations and/or failure to pass 
other requirements ten (10) days after the conduct of the final examination. 
 
STEPS IN THE APPLICATION FOR INC: 
 
a. Pay the required amount of fees to the School Cashier upon the consent of the 
instructor. 
b. Present the Official Receipt for the payment made to the School Registrar for 
the issuance of INC Form. 
c. Give the form to the instructor concerned. 
 
The instructor shall personally or through his/her authorized staff submits the 
accomplished INC Form to the School Registrar. If the instructor concerned is no 
longer working in the College, the Department Head may give the grade in behalf of 
the latter, provided the completion period should not exceed one semester from the 
time that such subject was enrolled. 
 
 
SECTION 7. RETENTION POLICY 
 
If during any term, a non-FHE student fails in one-third (1/3) of the subjects enrolled, 
he/she will be referred to the Guidance Counselor and may reenroll in the same 
curriculum upon a written recommendation by the Department Head after conferring with 
the Guidance Counselor. If a student flunks a second time, he/she shall not be allowed to 
reenroll in the same curriculum but he/she may enroll in another curriculum if accepted by 
the Department Head. If the student fails the third time, he/she shall be disqualified from 
further registration at TMC. If a student flunks in two or more subjects, he/she shall be de-
loaded with a unit based on his/her ability as suggested by the Guidance Counselor and 
approved by the Department Head.

and Regulations (IRR) of Republic Act 10931, more commonly known as the Free Higher 
Education Law, states that SUCs and LUCs are mandated to formulate and implement the 
Return of Service Agreements (RSA) system for students benefiting from the free higher 
education provision law as part of their admission and retention policies. 
 
The RSA maybe in a form of community immersion, outreach program, coastal clean-up, 
tree planting and other activities that the College President may deemed necessary. Also, 
students could render return service to the school by helping out as registration assistants 
or student assistants during the registration or enrollment period, volunteering during 
college-wide activities, and participating in outreach drives for victims of natural calamities 
organized by their schools, among others. 
 
Upon the recommendation of Deans/Department Heads the total number of hours to be 
rendered equivalent to total units of the curriculum enrolled by the students will be the

SECTION 9. GRADUATION 
 
Students who satisfactorily complied with all requirements for graduation will be conferred 
their respective degrees or titles under the following requirements: 
 
a. The candidate for graduation shall request for evaluation of his/her records within one 
(1) semester preceding his/her possible graduation to their respective Department 
Heads; 
b. Department Heads shall post the names of possible candidates for graduation in their 
respective bulletin boards; 
c. The candidate for graduation shall fill-up the Graduation Form and submit the same at 
the Registrar’s Office within one (1) semester preceding his/her possible graduation; 
d. Academic requirements for candidates for graduation which includes other fees 
pertaining to graduation must be completed three (3) weeks before the graduation 
ceremony; 
e. In case the student is a transferee, he must be a resident of the college for at least one 
year or two successive semesters regardless of the number of units enrolled to be 
qualified for graduation; 
f. All grades of graduating students must be completed prior to the graduation ceremony; 
and 
g. The candidate for graduation must be present at the commencement exercises and 
must wear the prescribed graduation attire during the commencement exercises.

Policy Declaration on Sexual Harassment 
 
In affirmation of the declaration of the policy set forth in Section 2 of 
Republic Act No. 7877, TMC shall guarantee full respect for human 
rights and uphold the dignity of workers, employees, applicants for 
employment, students including those undergoing on- the-job 
training, instruction or education. 
 
As a premier educational institution, TMC shall establish and maintain 
a moral, intellectual and healthy work environment in which the 
productivity and creativity of its workforce are nurtured, free from 
any and all kinds of sexual harassment. Towards this end, TMC 
commits itself to establishing and maintaining a “sexual harassment-
free” workplace and holds its faculty, students and employees to the 
highest degree of moral standards.

Definitions 
 
This policy uses the following definitions: 
 
1. “Student” means a person duly enrolled for any of the following: 
(a) a degree or course, (b) certificate course, or (c) cross-
registration in any of the academic units, institutes, or training 
centers of TMC. 
 
2. “Employee” means any person who holds an official appointment 
or designation, with or without remuneration; in any unit or 
office, academic or administrative; and includes casual, contractual 
or project employees of TMC; and 
 
3. “Faculty member” means any member of the teaching staff of 
TMC, regardless of academic rank or category and includes the 
librarian, guidance counselor, researcher or research associate, 
coach, trainer or training specialist, and extension worker. 
 
 
When Sexual Harassment is Committed 
 
“Sexual Harassment” is committed by an officer, employee, faculty 
member, coach, trainer, or any person who, having influence, moral 
ascendancy and authority over another demands, requests or 
otherwise requires any sexual favor from the other, regardless of 
whether such demand, request or requirement is accepted by the 
latter. This authority, influence or moral ascendancy may be in any 
aspect of academic or administrative work in any unit, office, institute, 
classroom, theater, playing court, training field or any other place 
within the premises and jurisdiction of TMC. 
 
Sexual harassment occurs when: 
 
1. The sexual favor is made as a condition for hiring, employment or 
reemployment of the individual who is the object of sexual 
harassment, or the basis for granting such individual favorable 
compensation, promotion or privileges or the refusal to grant the 
sexual favor results in discrimination or adversely affects such 
faculty member or employee; 
2. The person with authority, influence or moral ascendancy over 
another uses sexually suggestive language, graphics or action or 
employ physical contact with sexual undertones; 
3. The above acts impair the rights or privileges of the faculty 
member or employee under the Constitution, the Labor Code and 
other related rules and regulations; 
4. The above acts would result in an intimidating, hostile or offensive
[Page 56]
TMC Student Manual | Revised November 18, 2022 
 
 
56 | P a g e 
 
 
employment environment for the faculty member or employee. 
 
In the academic, teaching or study environment, sexual harassment 
is committed: 
 
1. Against a student, trainee or one who is under the care, custody, 
supervision or “advisorship” of the offender; 
2. Against one whose education, apprenticeship or tutorship is 
entrusted to the offender; 
3. When the sexual favor is used as a condition for the issuance of a 
passing grade, the granting of honors and scholarships, stipend, 
allowance or other benefits, privileges, or considerations; 
4. When a person with authority, influence or moral ascendancy 
over a student, trainee or apprentice, uses sexually suggestive 
language, graphics or action or employs physical contact with 
sexual undertones; or 
5. When sexual advances result in an intimidating, hostile or 
offensive academic environment for the student, trainee or 
apprentice.

The following guidelines shall be observed at all times within the 
premises of TMC and outside campus during an official school

6.6 
Thesis Writing 
 
Thesis writing subject in college refers to the reasons and justifications behind 
choosing a particular topic or theme related to the process of writing a thesis itself. 
Unlike a research-oriented thesis, a thesis writing subject focuses on exploring, 
analyzing, and presenting various aspects of the thesis writing process rather than 
conducting original research. 
 
Thesis writing subject should revolve around enhancing students' thesis writing 
experience, helping them develop essential academic skills, and supporting their 
overall academic and professional growth. By providing valuable insights, resources, 
and guidance, the chosen subject can contribute to the success of students' thesis 
endeavors. 
 
 
6.6.1 Guidelines for Thesis Advising in the Undergraduate Degree Programs 
 
All Thesis advisers must be a Faculty Member of TMC and must possess 
appropriate qualifications (Doctor’s degree). Advisers should familiarize 
themselves with the general College rules and their specific College’s rules 
governing advising students enrolled in the undergraduate thesis or other 
similar requirements (eg, special problem, a major paper, capstone project, 
group thesis, etc). 
 
While practices among different academic units may differ, these generally 
involve appointment of an adviser, a thesis proposal presentation, and a 
thesis defense. An undergraduate thesis should ideally be completed within a 
semester, as preliminary work is done at least one semester prior to 
enrolment in the thesis proper. 
 
The completion of a thesis within a reasonable length of time is the joint 
responsibility of both student and adviser. Both make a commitment to work 
together to ensure that the output will be of acceptable merit and 
scholarship and that it is completed on a date agreed upon. A good working 
relationship between the two parties is thus crucial to this effort.

SECTION 1: INSTITUTIONAL OVERVIEW
1.1 History
Trinidad Municipal College (TMC) was established in 1980 through the vision of Mr. Paciano Petarco, with the support of Municipal Mayor Atty. Avelino N. Puracan. Originally named Trinidad Junior College, it was registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in 1985 with SEC No. 136393. On August 1, 2003, the ownership and management were turned over to the Local Government of Trinidad under the leadership of Mayor Engr. Filadelfo A. Garcia. The institution was renamed Trinidad Municipal College (TMC).
1.2 Legal Basis
- Republic Act 10931 (Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act, UAQTEA)
- CHED Memorandum Orders (CMOs) on programs and standards
- Local Government Code of the Philippines
- TMC Charter and By-Laws
1.3 Recognition
TMC was recognized by the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) as a Higher Education Institution (HEI) pursuant to Commission en banc Resolution No. 564-2020 dated August 18, 2020.
1.4 Governance
TMC is an LGU-managed institution under the Municipality of Trinidad, Bohol. The College is headed by a College President who reports to the Municipal Mayor and the Sangguniang Bayan.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: VISION, MISSION, GOAL, PHILOSOPHY, AND SLOGAN
2.1 Vision
A model institution with fully developed academic, technical-vocational education and skill of manpower with positive work attitudes anchored in the core values of leadership and professionalism essential in the creation of self-reliant citizens.
2.2 Mission
To build well-trained, competent and employable professionals, who will meet the demands of the local and international work places.
2.3 Goal
TMC aims at evolving a whole individual as a child of God and a member of a democratic society who is professionally competent that can provide leadership and advance knowledge, well-trained in a certain vocation not only to help himself but to help others, and practical yet responsible and obedient to the laws of God and to the laws of the government.
2.4 Philosophy
TMC adheres to the philosophy that education is life and growth guided by faith in God and love of fellowmen in an environment of competitiveness, professionalism and excellence.
2.5 Slogan
TMC is committed to public educational services second to none.

SECTION 6: RETENTION AND ACADEMIC STANDING
6.1 Grading System
TMC uses the following grading system:
- 1.00 - 1.25: Excellent
- 1.26 - 1.50: Very Good
- 1.51 - 1.75: Good
- 1.76 - 2.00: Satisfactory
- 2.01 - 2.25: Fairly Satisfactory
- 2.26 - 2.50: Passing
- 2.51 - 3.00: Conditional
- 5.00: Failed
- INC: Incomplete
- DRP: Dropped
6.2 Retention Requirements
- Must maintain a General Weighted Average (GWA) of at least 2.00
- Must not have more than two (2) grades of 5.00 (Failed) in a semester
- Must not have been subjected to disciplinary action
- Must meet the specific retention requirements of the program
6.3 Academic Probation
- Students with a GWA below 2.00 are placed on academic probation
- Probationary students must improve their GWA within two (2) semesters
- Failure to improve results in academic dismissal
6.4 Academic Dismissal
- Students are academically dismissed if they:
  - Fail to improve their GWA after two (2) semesters of probation
  - Accumulate three (3) semesters of academic probation
  - Fail to meet the program's specific retention requirements
6.5 Re-admission After Dismissal
- Dismissed students may apply for re-admission after one (1) semester
- Re-admission is subject to approval by the Academic Affairs Office
- Re-admitted students are placed on probation for two (2) semesters

Trinidad Municipal College (TMC) was founded in 1980 when Mr. Paciano Petarco initiated the plan for establishing a college institution in the Municipality of Trinidad, Bohol. The institution was originally named Trinidad Junior College and was registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in 1985 with SEC No. 136393. On August 1, 2003, the ownership and management were turned over to the Local Government of Trinidad. The institution was renamed Trinidad Municipal College (TMC).

SECTION 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS
1.1 Purpose
This Faculty Manual establishes the policies, guidelines, and procedures governing the academic and professional conduct of all faculty members at Trinidad Municipal College (TMC). It serves as a reference for faculty rights, responsibilities, and performance standards.
1.2 Scope
This manual applies to all full-time, part-time, and adjunct faculty members across all colleges and departments of TMC, including the College of Teacher Education, College of Arts and Sciences, College of Computer Studies, College of Criminal Justice Education, and College of Office Administration.
1.3 Vision
TMC faculty are committed to being catalysts for academic excellence, producing graduates who are competent, competitive, and socially responsible professionals.
1.4 Mission
The faculty of TMC shall provide quality instruction, conduct meaningful research, and extend community service to produce well-rounded graduates who can contribute to local and national development.

SECTION 2: FACULTY QUALIFICATIONS
2.1 Minimum Qualifications
All faculty members must meet the following minimum qualifications:
- Bachelor's degree in the relevant field for instructor positions
- Master's degree in the relevant field for senior instructor positions
- Doctoral degree for professorial positions
- Must be a Filipino citizen or a foreign national with proper work authorization
- Must possess good moral character and no criminal record
- Must pass the TMC faculty selection process
2.2 Required Documents
Applicants must submit the following during application:
- Updated resume or curriculum vitae
- Transcript of records and diploma (original or certified true copy)
- Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) license, if applicable
- National Computer Center (NCC) certification, for IT faculty
- Certificate of employment from previous employers
- NBI or police clearance
- Medical certificate
- Two (2) character reference letters
2.3 Preferred Qualifications
- Teaching experience in higher education
- Industry experience relevant to the program being taught
- Published research papers or presentations
- Membership in professional organizations

RESEARCH MANUAL - TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE
Effective Date: Academic Year 2022-2023

SECTION 1: RESEARCH OVERVIEW
1.1 Purpose
This Research Manual establishes the policies, guidelines, and procedures for conducting research at Trinidad Municipal College (TMC). It ensures that research activities are conducted ethically, rigorously, and in compliance with national and institutional standards.
1.2 Research Philosophy
TMC believes that research is essential for institutional growth, faculty development, and student learning. Research activities should contribute to the advancement of knowledge, address community needs, and support the college's mission.
1.3 Research Objectives
- Promote a culture of research among faculty and students
- Generate new knowledge relevant to the local community and beyond
- Improve teaching and learning through evidence-based practices
- Contribute to national development through research and innovation
- Establish partnerships with other institutions and organizations
------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: RESEARCH ETHICS
2.1 Ethical Principles
All research at TMC must adhere to the following ethical principles:
- Respect for persons: participants must be treated with dignity and respect
- Beneficence: research must maximize benefits and minimize harm
- Justice: research must be fair and equitable
- Informed consent: participants must voluntarily agree to participate
- Confidentiality: participant information must be kept confidential
- Integrity: research must be conducted with honesty and transparency
2.2 Research Ethics Committee (REC)
- The REC is responsible for reviewing and approving all research involving human participants
- The REC is composed of five (5) members: chairperson, two (2) faculty representatives, one (2) external member, and one (2) community representative
- The REC meets at least once a month
- Research proposals must be submitted to the REC at least two (2) weeks before the scheduled meeting
2.3 Ethical Review Process
Step 1: Researcher submits the research proposal to the REC
Step 2: REC reviews the proposal for ethical compliance
Step 3: REC provides feedback or requests revisions
Step 4: Researcher revises the proposal as needed
Step 5: REC approves the proposal with an ethics clearance certificate
Step 6: Researcher conducts the research with ongoing ethical oversight
2.4 Informed Consent
- Participants must be provided with a consent form that includes:
  - Purpose and procedures of the research
  - Expected benefits and risks
  - Right to withdraw at any time
  - Confidentiality measures
  - Contact information of the researcher and REC
- Participants must sign the consent form before participating
- For minors, parental consent and minor assent are required
2.5 Risk Assessment
- Research involving vulnerable populations requires additional ethical review
- Research involving deception must be justified and must not cause harm
- Research involving sensitive topics requires additional confidentiality measures

- Page numbers: bottom center

BRIEF HISTORY:
The Vision to establish an affordable College in the Municipality of Trinidad was not realized overnight. It took many years of trials, hardships and collective efforts to bring forth what is now popularly known as TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE, one of the fastest growing Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Province of Bohol.
It started in 1980 when Mr. Paciano Petarco initiated the plan for the project of establishing a college institution in the town. He then consulted and sought the help of the Municipal Mayor that time, Atty. Avelino N. Puracan, who consequently assured him to look for competent financier/sponsor to start the project.
Incidentally, the Mayor meet a certain Antero Delos Reyes, a Davao-based educator, a businessman and owner of a private vocational school, in an occasion in Davao where the Mayor was invited as guest speaker by Trinidadnon Association in Davao. He requested Mr. Delos Reyes in putting up a branch of his private vocational school in Trinidad who eventually granted his request. The lone antiquated building in Trinidad, Bohol, used and inaugurated in 1957 as the original Municipal Hall of Trinidad was then converted in the year 1980 into this private school named as Trinidad Institute of Technology (TIT). TIT offered short-term vocational courses, such as Typewriting, Stenography, Tailoring and Bookkeeping.
However, TIT did not last longer than a semester as it “died a natural death”, so to speak. Earnest in their intention to establish a lasting college in the town that would offer affordable yet quality education to students form the lower income families not only from Trinidad but also from the neighboring town did not go in vain. It was in 1985 that Mayor Puracan and Mr. Petarco and the rest of the Municipal Officials at that time spearheaded some affluent Trinidadnons to form themselves as Incorporators, thereby instrumental in the recognition and registration of the TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE with the Securities and Exchange Commission in the same year 1985 with SEC No. 136393 using the same antiquated Municipal Hall as its school building, the same edifice vacated by Trinidad Institute of Technology.
On October 23, 1986, the Municipality of Trinidad donated the land including the
    building and the improvements thereon, to TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE which then
    offered the following courses, namely:
        1. 2 years Diploma in Applied Technology major in Electricity and Construction
        2. 2 years Associate in Secretarial Administration
        3. 2 years Automotive Mechanic Course
        4. 1 year General Clerical Course
In 1993, a Bachelor of Arts degree was approved by the Department of Education, Culture and Sports (DECS).
After more than a decade of moderate existence, TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE faced a pressing risk of extinction. Wise enough in finding a solution, the Incorporators came up with a redemptive decision in a meeting to turn over, by way of a DEED OF SALE (CONFIRMATION OF TRANSFER OF OWNERHIP), the Ownership, Management and Control of TRINIDAD JUNIOR COLLEGE to the Local Government of Trinidad under the leadership of then Municipal Mayor, Engr. Filadelfo A. Garcia, Vice Mayor Francisco U. Gonzales and the Sangguniang Bayan Members during their term on August 1, 2003.
The sale by the Incorporators effectively transformed Trinidad Junior College into what is now known as the TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE (TMC), from 1997 up to present, one of the fastest growing Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in the Philippines.
Before Mayor Judith Del Rosario-Cajes assumed as the newly elected mayor of Trinidad on June 30, 2007, Trinidad Municipal College has a total enrolment of only 510 students. TMC became a recipeint of RA 10931 otherwise known as Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Act (UAQTEA) on the 2nd semester of academic year 2018-2019. Trinidad Municipal College was recognized by the Commission of Higher Education (CHED) as Higher Education Institution (HEI) pursuant to Commission en banc Resolution No. 564-2020 dated August 18, 2020.
At present, under the dynamic, strong and creative leadership of Former Mayor JUDITH DEL ROSARIO CAJES, wife of former congressman, and now Mayor ATTY. ROBERTO C. CAJES, Ph.D., TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE (TMC) quickly and steadily started to evolve physically and academically to meet the standards of a dynamically changing world of education and employment. As of 1st Semester, Academic Year 2022-2023, TRINIDAD MUNICIPAL COLLEGE has a total enrolment of more than 7,000 students and offers the following courses, to wit:

VISION, MISSION, GOAL, PHILOSOPHY & SLOGAN:
TMC is guided with the following vision, mission and goal:
VISION: A model institution with fully developed academic, technical-vocational education and skill of manpower with positive work attitudes anchored in the core values of leadership and professionalism essential in the creation of self-reliant citizens.
MISSION: To build well-trained, competent and employable professionals, who will meet the demands of the local and international work places.
GOAL: TMC aims at evolving a whole individual as a child of God and a member of a democratic society who is professionally competent that can provide leadership and advance knowledge, well-trained in a certain vocation not only to help himself but to help others, and practical yet responsible and obedient to the laws of God and to the laws of the government.
PHILOSOPHY: TMC adheres to the philosophy that education is life and growth guided by faith in God and love of fellowmen in an environment of competitiveness, professionalism and excellence.
SLOGAN: TMC is committed to public educational services second to none.
